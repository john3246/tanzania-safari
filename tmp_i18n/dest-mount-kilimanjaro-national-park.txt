About Kilimanjaro National Park
  
Kilimanjaro National Park (Swahili: 
Hifadhi ya Taifa ya Kilimanjaro
) sits in Tanzania’s Kilimanjaro Region near Moshi, roughly 300 km south of the equator. The park protects the mountain above the tree line plus the encircling montane forest belt — a “sky island” of habitats from rainforest to arctic summit.
  
Quick facts climbers and safari guests ask most:
  
    
Summit:
 Uhuru Peak, 5,895 m (19,341 ft) on Kibo crater
    
Park area:
 ~1,688 km² / ~75,575 ha (UNESCO extent)
    
Established:
 National park 1973; UNESCO World Heritage 1987; forest expansion 2005
    
Managed by:
 Tanzania National Parks Authority (TANAPA)
    
HQ:
 Marangu — ~44 km from Moshi, ~86 km from Kilimanjaro International Airport (JRO)
    
Famous for:
 Non-technical Seven Summits trek, glaciers, Chagga foothills culture
  
  
Early 20th-century German colonial authorities declared the mountain and forests a game reserve; it became a forest reserve in 1921, then a national park above the tree line in 1973. In 2013 it was also named one of the Seven Natural Wonders of Africa.
  
    
    
Classic Kilimanjaro skyline above the savannah — the view that draws climbers from every continent.
  
  
Three Volcanic Peaks: Kibo, Mawenzi &amp; Shira
  
Kilimanjaro is one of the world’s largest volcanoes, formed from three cones:
  
    
      
Peak
What to know
      
        
Kibo
Highest cone; Uhuru Peak (5,895 m) sits on the crater rim. Standard trek target. Glaciers and ice fields are retreating with climate change.
        
Mawenzi
Rugged, technical summit (≈5,149 m). Not part of standard tourist treks; requires alpine climbing skills.
        
Shira
Collapsed western plateau; Shira Route and Lemosho approaches cross beautiful high moorland.
      
    
  
  
UNESCO highlights this combination — height, snow cap, isolation above the plains — as a superlative natural phenomenon (criterion vii).
  
Five Vegetation Zones (Equator to Arctic in a Week)
  
Ascending Kilimanjaro is like walking from the equator toward the poles. Zones change every day:
  
    
Cultivated lower slopes
 — Chagga farms, banana and coffee groves
    
Montane rainforest
 — mossy canopy, colobus monkeys, birds; critical water catchment
    
Heath &amp; moorland
 — giant heathers, lobelias, senecios (unique afro-alpine flora)
    
Alpine desert
 — sparse life, intense sun, freezing nights
    
Summit / arctic zone
 — rock, ice remnants, Uhuru Peak
  
  
These East African montane forests form part of a wider ecoregion valued for biodiversity and watershed protection — another reason the park’s forest belt matters beyond the summit selfie.

Wildlife in the Forests &amp; Moorland
  
Kilimanjaro is not a classic Big Five game drive park — the mountain itself is the star — but the montane forests and lower slopes hold real wildlife:
  
    
Forest:
 blue monkeys, eastern black-and-white colobus, bushbabies, leopards
    
Above timberline:
 Kilimanjaro tree hyrax, grey duiker, rodents; bushbuck and red duiker in places
    
Buffalo
 in forest (sometimes moorland); 
elephants
 between Namwai and Tarakia rivers and occasionally higher
  
  
For dense Big Five viewing, add 
Serengeti National Park
, 
Ngorongoro Crater
, or 
Tarangire
 after you descend.
  
Why Climb Kilimanjaro?
  
Tens of thousands of people attempt the summit each year. Common reasons — echoed across adventure writing and climber stories — include:
  
    
Accessible Seven Summit
 — no technical ropes on standard routes; age extremes from children to octogenarians have summited (fitness and altitude still decide outcomes)
    
Remote yet reachable
 — international flights into JRO; lodges and outfitters around Moshi / Arusha
    
Pristine trails
 — park rules weigh gear on/off the mountain to limit litter
    
Climate &amp; wonder
 — equatorial snow, shrinking glaciers, and afro-alpine plant life found on few peaks worldwide
    
Local livelihoods
 — guides, porters, cooks, and lodges support communities around the mountain
    
Uhuru (“Freedom”) Peak
 — summit name tied to Tanzania’s independence story and Julius Nyerere’s vision
  
  
Important:
 Altitude illness is real. Longer routes improve success rates. We brief every guest on “pole pole” pacing, hydration, and when to turn around safely.
  
Kilimanjaro Trek Costs &amp; Park Fees
  
Published retail climb prices vary widely by route length, group size, and camp standard. A private or small-group trek typically includes TANAPA conservation &amp; camping/hut fees, rescue fees, guide and porter wages, meals, and transfers.
  
    
Budget-conscious shared climbs:
 often from roughly mid–high hundreds to low thousands USD depending on days
    
Private quality climbs (6–8+ days):
 commonly mid-thousands USD per person — ask for a current quote
    
Park fees alone are a large share of cost; longer routes cost more but raise summit odds

How Many Days Do You Need?
  
    
Minimum honest trek:
 6 days (Machame / Rongai style) — 5-day pushes raise altitude risk
    
Recommended:
 7–8 days (Machame 7, Lemosho 7–8)
    
Best odds:
 Northern Circuit 8–9 days
    
Buffer:
 +1 arrival day in Moshi/Arusha, +1 recovery day before safari or flights
